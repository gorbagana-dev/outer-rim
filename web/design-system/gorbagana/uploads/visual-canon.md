## 2. Face system and expression

Faces are often flat circular/irregular patches inset into substantially more tactile bodies. They can be pale cream, gray, green, yellow or pink. Visible treatments include glossy kawaii eyes, concentric/rainbow eyes, star eyes, closed laughing or sleepy eyes, side-eye, spectacles, small fanged or toothy mouths, puckered lips and bubblegum.

Eyes, face color, expression and face-to-body scale are high-salience identity features. Keep them recognizable when adapting a specific character. Use the actual reference rather than assuming anime eyes or a grin for every example.

For the black/gold drawn eyes seen in #2702 and #565, specify irregular or angular upper contours, tapered lower shapes, black graphic fill, gold lower iris segments and small crisp white drawn highlights on a flat pale face patch. Inspect the chosen crop for its particular geometry. Do not translate the word “glossy” into spherical glass marbles, round plush-toy safety eyes or dimensional eyeballs. Photographic fur, plastic and dramatic lighting can coexist with deliberately flat eye and mouth artwork. This correction is specific to that eye family: preserve star eyes, rainbow eyes, side-eye, closed eyes and glasses when the selected character has them.

## 3. Materials and mixed media

The design tension matters: plastic, fur, crumpled bags, quilted slabs and packaging can be dimensional/tactile while the face and arms are flat cartoons. Photographic forearms, hands, cups, string games, boxing gloves and food can coexist deliberately with graphic faces and tiny pale noodle-like arms.

Do not smooth this into one uniform glossy 3D mascot, realistic goblin or vector sticker style by default. Conversely, do not mistake a low-resolution screenshot's blur/compression for an intentional artistic requirement. Background texture may be intrinsic; inspect other examples before generalizing a noise pattern.

## 4. Headwear, props and collage

Examples include ordinary/tilted can lids, green crowns, green/white caps, pink horns, green crocodile-shaped hats, purple drink-can headwear, foil-like caps and gray block-shaped lids. Photographic hands can hold string, drinks, pizza, a handheld game device or a paper-plane-like object. These are alternatives, not a mandatory checklist.

For exact identity, preserve salient headwear and signature props unless the brief changes them. For generic design, select one or two gag-relevant props. Do not accidentally inherit every secondary reference's accessories.

Small stickers and pop-cultural fragments may sit inside the original collage. They are optional artwork details, not proof of affiliation or an official Gorbagana logo. The green top-left clover and blue arrow/count controls are marketplace overlays: never treat them as headwear or logos.

## 5. Palette and staging

Green/black and green/blue combinations are prominent in these supplied examples, but beige, brown, gray, white and orange bodies are also reference-supported. Pink, purple, cyan and yellow often supply face/prop contrast. Follow a specific character's palette before a generalized brand palette.

PFPs usually prioritize a large central body and readable face against patterned, collage-like or environmental backgrounds. For memes and scenario art, retain those character cues but use the staging the scene requires. Full-body movement, action shots, wide scenes and dramatic lighting are allowed; the screenshot grid is a reference source, not an output layout.

For bin-based PFPs only, a loose layout is lower third/half can, middle fur/face, upper lid/headwear. Do not apply bin proportions to mattresses, capsules or stacked packaging.

## 6. Worldbuilding — creative proposals, not factual canon

Useful invented settings include dumpster alleys, landfill exchanges, gas depots, scrap-built server rooms, arcades, municipal council offices, garbage-truck infrastructure, scrap bridges, caution-tape checkpoints and parody sanitation bureaucracies. These are creative scene devices, not evidence that named institutions, infrastructure or lore actually exist.

The joke can be **serious infrastructure expressed through unserious garbage civilization**. It need not always be grimy: humor can be cute, uncanny, deadpan, grandiose, nostalgic or absurdly ceremonial.

## 8. Transformation test

A person/character adaptation should remain recognizable through a few selected cues—glasses, hair silhouette, collar, era, pose, object—while the resulting body belongs to a reference-supported family. A specific Gorbagio in a new scene must remain that character, not an averaged new mascot.

Before generation: name the family, the invariant traits, the permitted scene/style changes, the one focal gag and the interface elements to exclude. For exact edits, do not infer unseen or UI-covered details as fact.
